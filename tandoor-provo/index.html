The tandoor

A clay oven does things a pan can’t.

Ours is charcoal-fired and runs at roughly 480°C. Meat goes on the skewer after a full night in yogurt, ginger and Kashmiri chili; bread goes on the wall of the oven and comes off in about ninety seconds, blistered and still puffing. Everything marked From the Tandoor is cooked to order — nothing sits.

Our story

Cooked the way it’s cooked at home.

“A symphony of spices, where every dish is a celebration of authentic flavors and aromas.”

Most Indian restaurants in the United States pick a side. The north gets the tandoor, the cream and the wheat breads; the south gets rice, coconut and curry leaf. We never wanted to choose, so the menu carries both — a masala dosa fermented overnight can land on the same table as lamb chops off the charcoal.

Spices are ground in-house and bloomed in oil before anything else goes in the pan, because that is where the flavour actually comes from. Paneer is made here. Marinades sit a full day. Breads are pressed onto the oven wall to order, which is why the naan arrives after the curry and not before.

We cook to the heat you ask for, all the way up to Indian hot, and we’ll happily take the dairy or the gluten out of most dishes. Provo has been generous to us — 4.6 stars across several hundred reviews — and we’d rather earn the next one than coast on those.
