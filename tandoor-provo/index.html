The tandoor

A clay oven does things a pan can’t.

Our story

Cooked the way it’s cooked at home.

“A symphony of spices, where every dish is a celebration of authentic flavors and aromas.”

Most Indian restaurants in the United States pick a side. The north gets the tandoor, the cream and the wheat breads; the south gets rice, coconut and curry leaf. We never wanted to choose, so the menu carries both — a Mysore masala dosa can land on the same table as a lamb boti kebab off the skewer.

There are seventeen vegetarian mains, not the usual three — paneer six ways, okra, pumpkin, baby eggplant in coconut and sesame, peas in fenugreek cream. There is a Rajasthani laal maas and a Goan vindaloo on the same page. And there is a whole Indo-Chinese section, because that is what people actually eat in India.

Tell your server what you can and can’t eat and the kitchen will tell you straight what it can do. Provo has been generous to us — 4.6 stars across several hundred reviews — and we’d rather earn the next one than coast on those.
